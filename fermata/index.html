Overture

Fermata

noun, in music. A pause held for as long as it needs.

The pause goes where you point, and stays where you leave it.

Most interfaces talk over you. Fermata waits. It is a design system made of two typefaces, one colour and a great deal of space, for work that is read slowly and used for hours. There are no boxes to fill and no icons to decode. A checkbox is a sentence you strike through. A switch is the last word of a sentence, and you choose it. Everything the interface says is set upright in Archivo; everything you say back arrives in Bodoni italic, so your words are never mistaken for ours. Colour appears once, to show where you are. Motion answers you, then rests. What remains is the space between the notes, and that is where the reading happens.

A design system for type and silence

I

Principles

Five rules. Every component below follows all of them.

Silence is structure.

Space does the layout. A hairline appears only where space alone can't hold two things apart.

Type is the only ornament.

Weight, width, size, tracking and order carry every level of hierarchy. There are no icons, fills or shadows to fall back on.

Ours in roman, yours in italic.

The interface speaks upright, in Archivo. Whatever you choose or type answers in Bodoni italic, the way a score sets dolce apart from its notes.

One note of colour.

A single accent marks where you are: the current page, the chosen option, the focused field. The rest is ink and paper.

Nothing moves unless you do.

Motion answers a hand and then rests. The overture at the top is the one thing that plays by itself, once.

House rules

Sentence case. Capitals are for names and the first word. No labels in capitals, ever.

Italic is theirs. A chosen option, a typed value, a state someone set. Also musical and foreign terms, as books have always done.

Tracking follows size. Small text opens a little; display type closes in. Nothing is tracked for decoration.

Sixty-two characters. Body text holds its measure. Long lines belong to display type.

Figures that change sit still. Counters and times use tabular figures, so nothing jitters as it counts.

Real punctuation. Curly quotes, true apostrophes, and an en dash for ranges like 6–96.

III

Colour

Ink and paper do almost everything. One accent says where you are, crimson says something's wrong, and the highlighter is for reading.

Palette

Contrast is measured live against the paper, so it follows the key and Nocturne.

Loading

Three periods breathe in turn, only while something you started is still going. The button keeps its place and says what it's doing.

Toast

One line rises at the bottom left. The hairline under it is the time left; pointing at the toast pauses it. Undo is always in brackets.

  • --f-andante
  • --f-exhale

Dialog

The question, set large, with two answers in words. The safe answer is in brackets and takes focus first. Escape, or a click outside, keeps things as they were.

Errors

Say what happened and how to fix it, next to the field, in plain words. The line turns crimson. Nothing shakes and nothing apologises.

Most identity work runs four to eight weeks, and websites take a season. We agree the timeline before anything starts, and we keep to it.

Often. Some of the best briefs arrive before there's a name.

What do you need from us to begin?

A short brief, one person who can decide, and an hour for a first call.
